Aurchi Chowdhury

I am a senior-year Computer Science & Engineering student at the Bangladesh University of Engineering and Technology (BUET). My research interests span Representation Learning, Natural Language Processing, Multimodal AI, Computer Vision, and Mechanistic Interpretability.

I am broadly interested in how models build, organize, and use internal representations to reason: how language models encode and process linguistic structure, how visual encoders crystallize spatial and semantic information, and when a problem calls for it, how these representations come together across modalities.

To study these questions, I go deep into model architectures and representations to understand where and how knowledge is encoded, to make reasoning more robust where it breaks down, and to extend these capabilities to settings (languages, modalities, tasks) where they remain underexplored.

I am open to PhD opportunities for Fall 2027. 🎓

  1. ACL
    Causal Localization of the English Pivot in LLaVA: Mechanistic VLM Analysis and Training-Free Multilingual Steering
    In Proceedings of the 1st Workshop on Multilinguality in the Era of Large Language Models (MeLLM) at ACL 2026, Jul 2026
    Accepted · Archival
    Abs ACL Anthology Code

    Multilingual vision-language models (VLMs) consistently underperform on non-English visual queries, yet the internal mechanism behind this disparity remains unknown. As a focused case study on LLaVA-1.5-7B, we apply logit-lens analysis and causal activation patching to show that non-English visual queries are routed through an English-biased representational bottleneck in layers 5–17, extending the English-pivot phenomenon of Wendler et al. (2024) to the multimodal setting. Peak causal influence occurs at layer 8 (AIE=0.49, averaged across languages), with all measurable pivot signal running through text-token positions. Without meaningful visual content (blank-image condition), language-specific representations do not emerge at any layer, showing that the pivot is image-content-dependent rather than triggered by any visual input. Building on these findings, we derive training-free language-steering vectors at the mechanistically identified pivot layers, improving Russian VQA by +6.5 pp and Portuguese by +4.0 pp on MMMB without any fine-tuning — the latter surpassing the English baseline.

  2. CVPR
    Where Do Affordances Crystallize? Video vs. Image Self-Supervised Encoders for Embodied Perception
    In Proceedings of the 7th Annual Embodied AI (EAI) Workshop at CVPR 2026, Jun 2026
    Accepted · Non-archival · Poster
    Abs PDF Code

    Self-supervised video models are strong backbones for robotic perception. We probe 25 layer positions of architecturally matched ViT-L encoders (V-JEPA 2, V-JEPA 2.1, DINOv2) on the UMD Part Affordance Dataset and show that video SSL models crystallize affordance signal at layer 19 — four layers earlier than DINOv2 (image SSL, layer 23), suggesting a consistent effect of temporal predictive objectives on representation depth. Both V-JEPA variants share the same peak depth, yet V-JEPA 2.1’s intermediate-layer supervision yields markedly stronger early-layer representations (layer 2 mAP: 92.2 vs. 85.0 for V-JEPA 2). Head ablation at each peak layer further reveals a sparse set of attention heads that concentrate the affordance signal; keeping only the top-3 heads achieves a 5.3× attention compute reduction with ≤0.1 mAP loss.

Bengali Long-Form ASR and Diarization

Speech Recognition · Speaker Diarization · Low-Resource NLP

  • Built for a Kaggle competition on Bengali long-form ASR and Diarization, tackling low-resource Natural Language Processing for a language with no off-the-shelf speech tooling.
  • For ASR, built WhisperAlign, a word-boundary-aware chunker that extracts per-word timestamps from Whisper's own cross-attention alignment heads (no external tools or boundary annotations needed) and greedily assembles fixed-length segments that never break mid-word; used the resulting chunks to fine-tune a Whisper-medium Bengali ASR model.
  • For diarization, domain-adapted Pyannote for Bengali speaker diarization by fine-tuning only its segmentation head on Bengali conversational audio, teaching the model local speech rhythm and turn-taking patterns without disturbing the language-agnostic embedding space; paired it with a Dual-VAD intersection (Pyannote ∩ Silero VAD) that retains only segments confirmed by an independent speech boundary detector.

MemoryLane

Automatic Image Colorization · Computer Vision · Generative Models

Approached grayscale image colorization as a Computer Vision problem of conditional image generation in the CIELab color space, building a ConvNeXt-Base encoder–U-Net decoder with a PatchGAN discriminator. Reduced the dull, grayish outputs common in colorization models by training on colorfulness-filtered data with a combined perceptual + adversarial loss (VGG + PatchGAN).
